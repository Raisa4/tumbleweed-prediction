## Project name
Tumbleweed Trend Detective

## High-level summary (short)
In the dry western US, wind rolls tumbleweeds into neighborhoods and highways, burying homes and closing roads. Cities and road crews are always caught by surprise. We built a tool that pulls 25 years of NASA Earth-science data for three real pile-up hotspots, covering spring rainfall, vegetation greenness, autumn soil moisture, windy days and open land. It tests each one for a statistically significant trend (Mann-Kendall + Sen's slope) and combines them into a Tumbleweed Conditions Index. We check the index against real news-reported pile-ups and map where wind pushes the plants against buildings. Satellites can't see individual tumbleweeds; we show the conditions they need, honestly.

## NASA data used
- GPM IMERG monthly precipitation (GPM_3IMERGM): spring rainfall
- MODIS Terra vegetation indices, 16-day 250 m (MOD13Q1 v061): peak greenness
- GLDAS Noah land surface model, monthly 0.25° (SoilMoi0_10cm_inst): autumn topsoil moisture
- MERRA-2 hourly single-level diagnostics (M2T1NXSLV, U10M/V10M): windy days and wind direction
- MODIS land cover type, yearly 500 m (MCD12Q1 v061, LC_Type1): open land fraction
- Accessed via NASA Earthdata (earthaccess) and AppEEARS `[TODO: confirm with Person A, add Giovanni if used]`

## Other data
- GBIF and iNaturalist occurrence records (Salsola / Kali, Bassia scoparia)
- OpenStreetMap (land use, buildings, fences) via osmnx
- News reports of pile-up events (links in `data/*/events.csv`)

## Methods
- Mann-Kendall trend test, Hamed–Rao modification for autocorrelation (`pymannkendall`); significance at p < 0.05
- Sen's slope with 95% confidence interval as the rate of change
- Index: z-score of each variable (soil moisture sign flipped), averaged
- Backtest: rank of the index in the season before each reported pile-up
- Pile-up map: 250 m grid; source weights pushed downwind up to 4 km by 8-direction wind frequency above a user-chosen threshold, accumulating at barriers

## Limitations (we state these in the app too)
- Satellites model conditions and habitat, not individual plants
- MERRA-2 wind is ~50 km resolution
- Wind threshold for tumbling is an assumption (slider 8–16 m/s)
- Backtest covers ~8 events: plausibility, not accuracy
